## What this is

A 35-section reference covering prion diseases — their biology, history, diagnosis, epidemiology, regulation, and the open questions the field has not resolved.

It began as a plain-language explainer and grew into something closer to a critical review. The distinguishing feature is not coverage but **sourcing discipline**: every claim was checked against the paper it came from rather than against a summary of it, and the corrections that produced are recorded rather than quietly absorbed.

Written for a reader without a biology background. Terminology is defined on first use and collected in a glossary.

## Why it might be useful

Three things in it are not available assembled elsewhere:

**§18 — Presence and damage are decoupled.** Five findings from three separate sections, each discovered independently, all pointing at the same thing: where prion protein is and where neurons die do not match. The section separates four distinct phenomena that had been collapsed into one, and traces what each means for diagnosis, surveillance interpretation, and drug targets.

**§19 — How this literature loses its qualifications.** A documented pattern of qualified findings becoming unqualified claims in transmission, consistently toward the cleaner version. Six instances traced link by link, plus a subsection on retrieval-layer failures — including a publisher serving a different article under a correct DOI, caught only by an independent registry check.

**§24 — The experiment that would settle it.** The UK appendix survey positives have never been shown infectious or non-infectious. The section establishes that the question is partly foreclosed by how the specimens were preserved, specifies what could still be done, and documents that the enabling experiment has been funded since 2022 and never reported.

## How it was built

Sections were researched one at a time across separate sessions, then audited. The audit opened every cited source and checked it against the claim attached to it — roughly 345 claims across twelve sections in the first pass, with later sections audited as they were added.

Three rules did most of the work:

- **Open the page.** A search snippet is not a source. Where a page could not be opened, the document says so and names what was tried.
- **A claim in a brief is a hypothesis, not a premise.** Framings supplied in a research request are tested, not built on. Several were wrong, and §19 records them alongside the literature's own errors.
- **Report absences as findings.** Where nobody has done an experiment or asked a question, that is stated with the search recorded, rather than filled in with something adjacent.

Every correction is logged in the Verification tab with what it was before and what the source actually says. Claims resting on unread sources are marked as such in the body, not only in the citations.

## What it is not

Not peer-reviewed. Not medical advice. Not a substitute for the primary literature — it is a map of it, with the gaps drawn in.

Several figures are live surveillance numbers carrying as-of dates. Some claims rest on sources that could not be opened; those are marked. The Verification tab lists what remains outstanding.

## Reading it

The document is hosted as a live artifact at the link above. In-document cross-references between sections work there; exported copies (PDF, Word, Markdown) keep the text but not the internal navigation.

Start with §1 for the mechanism, §2 for the disease taxonomy, §6 for terminology. §35 maps the open questions by how answerable each is — questions with a specific experiment that would settle them, questions that are contested interpretations of existing evidence, and questions nobody currently knows how to approach.
